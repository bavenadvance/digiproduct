# Baven Advance: Brand Positioning & Tone of Voice
**Context file for the AI website builder. Apply to every page, section, button and microcopy.**

---

## 1. Positioning (the one idea behind every line)

**Baven Advance is an AI partner, not an AI agency.**

Agencies deliver a project and leave. A partner shares the goal, owns the outcome and keeps improving it. Every sentence on the site should make the reader feel they'd have someone in their corner, not a vendor to manage.

**Positioning statement:**
> For lenders, clinics and local trades businesses, Baven Advance is the AI partner that finds where AI makes you money, builds it, and stays to make it better every month. Unlike generic agencies, we specialise in your industry and stay accountable for results.

**Core promise:** Advantage that compounds.
**Brand line:** *Not an agency. Your AI partner.*

### The partner shift (use this to check any copy)
| Agency language (avoid) | Partner language (use) |
|---|---|
| We deliver AI solutions | We grow with you |
| Our services | How we work with you |
| Submit an enquiry | Let's talk |
| Project complete | Now it gets better |
| Clients | The businesses we back |
| We implement | We build it with you, then we stay |

## 2. Tone: Bold & Innovative, with a partner's heart

**Four tone pillars**

1. **Bold.** Short, confident, decisive. We take positions. We say what we think.
2. **Forward-looking.** Energy and momentum. The future is arriving, so let's get ahead of it.
3. **Plain-spoken.** Smart without jargon. Say "answers every call" and not "leverages conversational AI."
4. **On your side.** Always "we" and "you". We back the reader, we don't sell at them.

**We sound like:** a sharp, energetic co-founder who happens to know AI inside out.
**We never sound like:** a corporate brochure, a hype-merchant, or a robot.

---

## 3. Voice rules

- **Lead with the outcome, not the tech.** Booked jobs, recovered leads, hours back. AI is the how, never the headline.
- **Short sentences. Strong verbs.** Aim for under 20 words. Break rhythm with fragments. Like this.
- **Use "we" and "you."** Avoid "the company", "clients" and "users."
- **One idea per line.** Then give it room.
- **Confidence without hype.** Bold claims need a proof point or a placeholder for one. No invented numbers.
- **Honest about limits.** Telling people what *not* to automate builds more trust than promising everything.
- **Active voice, present tense.** "We build it" beats "it will be built."
- **Name the pain, then flip it.** Show the cost of standing still, then the advantage.

### Words to use
advantage, partner, momentum, compound, ahead, backed, built with you, together, results, every month, on your side, unfair advantage (sparingly)

### Words to avoid
leverage, synergy, cutting-edge, revolutionary, game-changing, solutions, state-of-the-art, end-to-end, holistic, "AI-powered" as filler, "we are passionate about", anything that could be pasted onto any competitor's site

## 5. Copy patterns to reuse

**The flip:** "*[Pain the reader feels].* *[What we do instead].*"
> Missed calls cost you jobs. Now every one gets answered.

**The partner line:** "*We [verb] it. Then we stay.*"
> We build it. Then we stay.

**The honest edge:** "*We'll tell you [uncomfortable truth], because [benefit to reader].*"
> We'll tell you what not to automate, because that's what protects your ROI.

**The triple:** three short beats, ending on the strongest.
> Faster replies. Fuller calendars. Compounding results.

**The specialist flex:** "*[Industry] is all we do.*"

## 6. Page-by-page voice guidance

| Section | Job | Tone note |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Stop the scroll, claim the partner position | Boldest line on the site. Max ~12 words. |
| Problem | Make them feel seen | Direct, a little blunt, never condescending |
| Who we are | Prove we're partners, not vendors | Warm, human, "we're in it with you" |
| Audience sections | Speak each industry's language | Use their terms: pipeline, no-shows, jobs booked |
| Why us | Specialist authority | Confident, specific, zero fluff |
| Process | Reduce risk | Calm, clear, step by step |
| Results | Build belief | Facts first. Only real numbers. Use placeholders until supplied |
| FAQ | Remove doubt | Conversational and honest |
| Final CTA | Create momentum | Short, energetic, low pressure |

**Rhythm tip:** alternate big bold lines with calmer supporting lines, so the page breathes.

---

## 7. Do / Don't checklist for the builder

**Do**
- Open sections with the benefit or the pain, never the feature.
- Use "partner" and "with you" language throughout.
- Keep paragraphs to 1 to 3 lines.
- Make every CTA the same action: **Book a Discovery Call.**
- Mark unknown stats or quotes as `[PLACEHOLDER]` until real ones are supplied.

**Don't**
- Invent statistics, testimonials, logos or guarantees.
- Use jargon (LLM, RAG, orchestration) without a plain-English translation.
- Overpromise ("100% automated", "guaranteed 10x").
- Use stock phrases that fit any agency.
- Use exclamation marks in body copy. Energy comes from the writing, not punctuation.

## 8. Quick self-test (run before publishing any line)
1. Would a competitor be able to say this word-for-word? If so, rewrite it.
2. Does it sound like a partner or a vendor?
3. Is the outcome before the technology?
4. Could a plumber, a lender and a clinic owner all understand it on first read?
5. Is every claim real or clearly placeholdered?
